# Source Quality

Use this reference when researching US stocks for the Gotrade skill. The goal is
to separate durable facts from market commentary, speculation, and stale data.

## Source Priority

Prefer primary sources for company-specific facts:
- Company investor relations pages
- Earnings releases and shareholder letters
- SEC filings such as 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, S-1, and proxy statements
- Earnings call transcripts and prepared remarks
- Official exchange, regulator, or government releases

Use reputable secondary sources for context:
- Reuters, AP, Bloomberg, CNBC, Wall Street Journal, Financial Times, Barron's
- Yahoo Finance, Nasdaq, MarketWatch, Morningstar, Koyfin, Finviz, TradingView
- Analyst-rating aggregators such as TipRanks, MarketBeat, Benzinga, or broker
  summaries when the source date is visible

Treat these as lower confidence unless corroborated:
- Blogs, newsletters, YouTube, Reddit, X/Twitter, Discord, forums
- SEO finance pages with no visible author, date, or source trail
- Unsourced AI summaries or pages that only repeat other pages
- Promotional investor content, stock-picking ads, or sponsored analysis

## Evidence Rules

- Use the most recent available facts, but identify the reporting period.
- Distinguish actual reported results from management guidance.
- Distinguish analyst opinion from company-provided data.
- Mention whether market prices, analyst ratings, and valuation data may be
  delayed.
- Prefer at least two independent sources for major claims.
- If sources disagree, explain the disagreement instead of forcing certainty.
- If a fact is material to the verdict, include the source name and date.

## Red Flags In Sources

Be cautious when:
- The source has no date or relies on outdated financials.
- The article discusses price movement without explaining a business driver.
- A bullish or bearish claim has no data behind it.
- The page mixes news with affiliate brokerage promotions.
- The article focuses on short-term price targets without fundamentals.

## Citation Style

In the final output, keep citations light and readable:
- "According to the company's Q1 2026 release..."
- "Reuters reported on May 5, 2026..."
- "Yahoo Finance showed delayed market data as of..."
- "Analyst consensus from MarketBeat/TipRanks was..."

Do not overload the user with links unless they ask for a deeper research pack.
