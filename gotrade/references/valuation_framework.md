# Valuation Framework

Use this reference when deciding whether a stock looks cheap, fair, expensive,
or unclear. Valuation is sector-specific and should never rely on one metric.

## Core Metrics

P/E ratio:
- Most useful for profitable, mature companies.
- Less useful for cyclical earnings, temporarily depressed earnings, or companies
  reinvesting heavily.

Forward P/E:
- Useful when earnings are expected to change quickly.
- Risk: depends on analyst forecasts that can be revised sharply.

PEG ratio:
- Useful for profitable growth companies.
- Risk: growth assumptions may be too optimistic.

Price/sales:
- Useful for high-growth or low-profit companies.
- Risk: ignores margins, dilution, and cash burn.

Price/free cash flow:
- Useful for mature businesses with meaningful cash generation.
- Risk: free cash flow can be temporarily inflated or depressed by working
  capital and capex cycles.

EV/EBITDA:
- Useful when comparing companies with different debt levels.
- Risk: ignores capex and stock-based compensation.

Dividend yield:
- Useful for REITs, utilities, telecoms, energy majors, and dividend ETFs.
- Risk: high yield can signal distress, rate sensitivity, or a pending cut.

## Interpretation Rules

- Compare valuation to the company's history, sector peers, and growth profile.
- A low multiple can mean value, cyclicality, weak growth, or real distress.
- A high multiple can be justified by durable growth, margins, and returns on
  capital, but it raises execution risk.
- For unprofitable companies, focus on revenue growth, gross margin, cash burn,
  runway, dilution, and path to profitability.
- For capital-intensive companies, check capex needs and debt maturity risk.
- For cyclical companies, avoid extrapolating peak margins into the future.
- For mega-cap compounders, valuation risk often comes from multiple compression
  even when the business remains strong.

## Quick Status Labels

Cheap:
- Valuation is below reasonable peers or history, and fundamentals are stable.

Fair:
- Valuation broadly matches growth, margins, balance sheet, and risk.

Expensive:
- Valuation requires strong execution or optimistic growth assumptions.

Unclear:
- Data is stale, earnings are distorted, company is unprofitable, or peer
  comparisons are weak.

## Red Flags

- Revenue growth slowing while valuation remains high.
- Margin pressure with no clear recovery path.
- Rising debt, dilution, or cash burn.
- One-time gains making earnings look stronger than operations.
- Dividend yield high because the share price collapsed.
- Stock-based compensation materially diluting shareholders.
