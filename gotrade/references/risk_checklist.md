# Risk Checklist

Use this reference before forming the Gotrade research signal mix. The user may
ask for a stock view, but the trade decision depends on portfolio context,
position size, time horizon, and risk tolerance.

## Pre-Trade Questions

If the user did not provide the answer, do not block the analysis. Mark it as
"not provided" and remind them to check it before trading.

- What is the time horizon: days, months, or years?
- Is this a new position, add, hold, trim, or exit?
- How large will the position be after the trade?
- Will the trade increase concentration in one stock, sector, theme, or factor?
- Is the user averaging down because the thesis is intact or because of loss
  aversion?
- Would the user still hold the stock if it dropped another 20-30%?
- Is there a near-term event such as earnings, CPI/Fed decision, litigation,
  product launch, or regulatory decision?
- Is the trade based on business fundamentals or only price movement?

## Gotrade-Specific Checks

- Check Gotrade for the live quote before placing an order.
- Confirm whether the quote is during regular trading hours or extended hours.
- Confirm available cash and whether the trade will use fractional shares.
- Consider USD exposure and FX conversion impact.
- Consider platform fees, spread, and order type.
- For non-US residents, consider local tax rules and US withholding taxes.
- Dividend withholding can materially affect income strategies.

## Portfolio Concentration Prompts

Use these prompts when the user already owns related positions:
- "This adds to your existing AI/semiconductor exposure."
- "This overlaps with your data-center infrastructure exposure."
- "This adds more real estate / REIT interest-rate sensitivity."
- "This adds more energy or power-price exposure."
- "This adds more defense / space volatility."
- "This position may already be represented inside your ETFs."

## Trade Type Guidance

For a new buy:
- Check whether the stock fills a portfolio gap or duplicates existing exposure.
- Prefer a starter-size framing when uncertainty is high.

For adding:
- Check whether the original thesis still holds.
- Compare the current valuation and news to the original reason for buying.

For trimming:
- Identify whether the reason is risk control, valuation, thesis change, or cash
  need.
- Do not treat trimming as a negative view by default.

For selling:
- Look for thesis break, better opportunity, concentration risk, or permanent
  impairment.
- Avoid framing a sale only around short-term price weakness.
